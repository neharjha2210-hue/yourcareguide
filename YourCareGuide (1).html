Recovery continues at home

Leaving the hospital is an important step. Following your discharge instructions, taking medicines correctly, attending follow-ups and knowing when to seek help can support a smoother recovery.

Before You Leave the Hospital

Make sure you and your caregiver understand the discharge plan.

📄

Understand your instructions

Know your diagnosis, treatment plan, activity restrictions, diet instructions and what symptoms should be reported.

💊

Review your medicines

Confirm each medicine, its purpose, dose, timing and duration. Ask what to do if you miss a dose.

📅

Plan follow-up care

Keep your appointment details and know which healthcare professional or department to contact with questions.

Care at Home

Every patient's recovery is different. Follow your own discharge instructions first.

1

Rest & sleep

Allow your body time to recover. Maintain a comfortable sleep routine and avoid activities your healthcare professional has restricted.

2

Food & hydration

Follow the recommended diet. Drink fluids according to your medical instructions, especially if you have fluid restrictions.

3

Personal & wound care

Keep yourself and any wound or incision clean and cared for exactly as instructed. Do not apply unapproved products.

4

Activity

Return to activity gradually and only within the limits given at discharge. Ask before restarting strenuous exercise or driving.

Medication Safety

A simple routine can help prevent missed doses and confusion.

🕐

Follow the schedule

Take medicines exactly as prescribed. Do not change the dose or stop a prescribed medicine without professional advice.

📝

Keep a medicine list

Record the medicine name, dose and timing. Keep the list available for appointments and emergencies.

⚠️

Ask before adding medicines

Check with a healthcare professional before taking new OTC medicines, supplements or herbal products.

Know the Warning Signs

Do not ignore a serious or rapidly worsening change in your condition.

🚨 Seek prompt medical help when appropriate

Depending on the patient's condition, concerning symptoms may include difficulty breathing, chest pain, fainting, severe or rapidly worsening pain, persistent vomiting, significant confusion or weakness, high/persistent fever, or concerning changes around a wound such as increasing redness, swelling or drainage.

If symptoms are severe or life-threatening, contact your local emergency service immediately. Do not use this website to diagnose an emergency.

Caregiver Guide

Family members and caregivers can help make recovery more organized and safe.

👨‍👩‍👧

Support daily needs

Help with meals, appointments, medicines and daily activities when the patient needs assistance.

👀

Observe changes

Pay attention to new or worsening symptoms and communicate concerns to the healthcare team.

🤝

Communicate

Keep discharge papers accessible and write down questions or symptoms to discuss at follow-up visits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I follow general advice instead of my discharge instructions?

No. Your individual discharge instructions are more important because they are based on your condition and treatment.

What should I do if I am unsure about a medicine?

Do not guess. Contact your healthcare professional or pharmacist and keep your discharge/medication list available.

When should I attend follow-up?

Attend the date and time provided in your discharge plan. If you are unsure, contact the hospital or healthcare professional who arranged your follow-up.

Can YourCareGuide diagnose my symptoms?

No. YourCareGuide is an educational project and cannot replace professional medical assessment or personalized medical advice.
